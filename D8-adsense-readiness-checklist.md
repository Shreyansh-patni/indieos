# D8 — IndieOS AdSense Readiness Checklist

- **Site:** IndieOS — https://indieos.tech · **Application target:** January 2027 (site launch target 31 Oct 2026)
- **Date checked for every official source below:** 2026-10-23
- **Source rule:** Section 1 contains only requirements traceable to official Google AdSense Help, Google Publisher Policies, or Google AdSense Program Policies pages. Section 2 is clearly separated and contains recommendations that are **not** official requirements.
- **No approval guarantees:** Nothing in this checklist — completing every row included — guarantees AdSense approval. Google reviews the applicant's account details **and** the whole site against its policies, and approval is Google's decision. Google's own framing is two-stage: “**Account approval:** We review the publisher's payments details… **Site approval:** We review the publisher's site to make sure it complies with the AdSense Program policies… Both the account and site need to be approved by AdSense before ads can start showing.” (Connect/add-site guidance and account-approval documentation, §sources.)

### Application-day runbook (sequence distilled from Section 1)

1. Renew `indieos.tech` (expires Feb 2027) → confirm rows 1–22 pass on the live site → re-check policy pages (row 27).
2. Create/sign in to AdSense with the correct Google Account → complete account and payment details accurately (row 4).
3. Sites → Add site `indieos.tech` → connect via the code-snippet method (code in `<head>` of ad-bearing pages) or another method Google offers in the account (row 3) → Verify → Request review → select the CMP option presented (row 13 groundwork).
4. Wait the stated window (a few days, sometimes 2–4 weeks, row 23). Do not click your own ads, buy traffic, or restructure the site meanwhile.
5. On approval: publish `/ads.txt` with the exact supplied line (row 21) → implement ad units per row 25 → run the reading-experience test (row 20) and the EU-consent test (row 13) → then announce nothing about ads to readers beyond the Privacy Policy and consent message (row 16 forbids drawing attention to ads to solicit clicks).
6. On rejection: read the stated reason, fix the underlying issue (row 24), log the fix, then request review from the Sites page. Never resubmit unchanged.

## Section 2 — Recommendations (NOT official requirements)

> Everything in this section is IndieOS best practice or widely repeated practitioner advice. **None of it is a Google requirement**, and meeting or missing any item here neither qualifies nor disqualifies the site by itself.

**The explicit no-minimum finding.** I checked the official AdSense eligibility page, the “make sure your site's pages are ready” page, the AdSense Program Policies and the Google Publisher Policies on **2026-10-23**. **Google publishes no minimum number of posts, no minimum word count per article, no minimum traffic level, and no minimum domain age for AdSense approval in those official pages.** The eligibility page's substantive content test is qualitative — content that is “high-quality, original, and attract an audience” — and the readiness/insufficient-content guidance is likewise qualitative (“enough text” for review and crawling; complete sentences and paragraphs rather than only headlines). Numbers circulating in forums and third-party guides (for example “50 posts”, “800–1,200 words”, “post for 6 months”, “10% approval rate”) appear in **community-forum posts and non-Google sites, not in Google's official eligibility or policy documentation**, and are **not** used anywhere in Section 1. Treat any such number below as an IndieOS planning choice, never as a threshold Google set.

Recommendations for IndieOS (planning choices, labelled as such):

| Recommendation | Why IndieOS adopts it (best practice, not a rule) |
|---|---|
| Apply with a real back catalogue rather than at launch — target the planned Jan 2027 date, roughly two months of live publishing after the 31 Oct 2026 launch | Gives reviewers (and readers) a finished, populated site across all four pillars instead of a launch-week skeleton; also de-risks the “under construction / insufficient content” failure patterns in row 6/24, which are qualitative judgements a fuller site answers better |
| Editorial depth target per article: cover the reader's problem completely, including steps, examples and failure cases, however long that honestly takes | Mirrors Google's helpful-content guidance for Search (people-first, complete, first-hand) — a Search quality standard IndieOS adopts editorially; it is **not** an AdSense word-count rule, and padding to a number is explicitly against that same guidance |
| Steady publishing cadence (e.g. a sustainable weekly rhythm the solo founder can actually keep) | A visibly maintained site with accurate `lastmod` dates and no stale “under construction” corners reads as established; cadence itself is not assessed against any published Google schedule |
| Trust pages beyond the required Privacy Policy: About, Contact, Editorial Policy, Terms, author archives (specified in D6 §6) | They make the publisher identifiable and the content accountable, which supports the qualitative originality/quality review and reader trust. **They do not guarantee approval**, and Google does not publish a required trust-page list beyond the Privacy Policy disclosure in row 12 |
| Connect Google Search Console and keep the site indexed and error-free before applying | A clean indexing record makes the “fully built and launched” state (row 6) easy for any reviewer or crawler to confirm; Search Console verification can also serve as a site-connection method (row 3). It is a practical aid, not a stated approval condition |
| HTTPS everywhere, fast pages, no broken links at application time | Page-experience quality supports both Search performance and the row-7 usability impression; Google does not publish a Core Web Vitals pass mark for AdSense |
| Prepare the CMP decision in December 2026 (choose a Google-certified CMP, draft the consent copy, update the Privacy Policy draft) so row 13 is a configuration task in January, not a research task during the review window | Sequencing convenience only |
| Keep a dated evidence folder for the application: screenshots of the live trust pages, the policy self-audit (Section 1 rows initialled), and the ads.txt fetch after approval | Makes any rejection response (row 24) specific and fast; Google does not ask for such a folder |
